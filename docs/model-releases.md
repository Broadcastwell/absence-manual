# AI model release tracker: what changed in each engine?

This AI model release tracker lists the model releases that the providers behind ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode dated on their own public pages between 1 July and 9 November 2026. Each row gives the release in the provider's words, the date the provider published and the page we read. A release is a dated mark on our time series, never an explanation of a change in answers.

Updated 9 November 2026.

We read every source below on 9 November 2026. Release names are quoted as each provider wrote them. Where a provider names the plans or surfaces a release reaches, we repeat that scope; where it does not, we say so rather than guess. We make no forecast of future releases.

## How we mark a release on our time series

These are our annotation rules, in force from 9 November 2026. They apply to every chart and table that compares two captures.

1. **Provider-dated only.** We mark a release only when the provider's own page dates it. A report, a screenshot or a leak is not marked.
2. **Every mark carries six fields:** the provider, the release in the provider's words, the date the provider published, the source URL, the date we read it, and the scope the provider states.
3. **Three mark types.** A *model release* names a new model. A *default change* says a model became the default for a plan. A *product change* alters an answer surface without naming a model, such as the Google post of 15 October 2026.
4. **Placement.** A mark sits on the date the provider published. When the provider describes a rollout window, such as "this week", we record that window in words and do not guess an end date.
5. **Between captures.** When a mark falls between two capture dates, the comparison carries a note naming the mark. We never attribute a change in a named count to a release. A change is reported only where the two 95 percent intervals separate, under our [published method](https://broadcastwell.com/methodology).
6. **Plans are not our setting.** Our captures are signed-out, single-turn answers in US English. A release scoped to a paid plan does not tell us what a signed-out answer uses, so we record the stated scope and infer nothing further.
7. **Corrections.** If a provider edits a release note, we keep our first reading with its date and add the new reading beside it.
