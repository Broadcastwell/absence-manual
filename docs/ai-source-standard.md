# AI source attribution: what a buyer report can establish

An AI answer mentioning a company, a visitor arriving from a source and a qualified opportunity are different observations. A useful business report keeps their evidence and dates separate. The AI Source Standard's original published v1.0 remains in its [7 October 2026 research record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23225776). This page explains how a buyer reads the resulting evidence.

<span id="1-form-fields"></span>
## What did the buyer report?

A buyer may say an AI assistant helped them find or assess a business. Treat that statement as self-reported information, with the date and the question the buyer chose to share. It is useful evidence of the buyer's account, rather than proof of a specific browser visit. An unanswered source question means the source was not supplied.

<span id="2-crm-properties-and-mapping"></span>
## What does the business record establish?

A contact or opportunity record can establish that a defined event occurred within a reporting window. State what counted as an enquiry, a qualified opportunity or a completed sale, and who applied the definition. Keep the buyer's source statement separate from the recorded commercial event. Do not turn a visibility observation into an inferred opportunity value.

<span id="3-browser-referrer-and-parameter-classes"></span>
## What does a referral establish?

A received referral or campaign label is an observation about a visit. It may be incomplete, absent or ambiguous. It does not identify every influence on the buyer. A copied link can carry a label into a different setting, and a buyer can return directly after using an assistant. Report the evidence received and leave the unknown portion visible.

<span id="4-analytics"></span>
## How should the report show visits?

State the reporting period and the visit definition. Keep observed source labels separate from the buyer's self-report, and explain when the source cannot be distinguished. A change in visits does not automatically establish a change in qualified demand. Compare periods with consistent definitions and disclose changes that affect interpretation.

<span id="5-server-user-agents"></span>
## Does a crawler request count as a buyer visit?

No. A crawler accessing a page is a different event from a person visiting it or an assistant citing it in an answer. Read the [bot-access guide](resources/bot-access.md) for the distinction between permission to read, search eligibility and an observed citation. Keep these event types out of a shared customer-outcome total.

<span id="6-reporting-definitions"></span>
## Which measures belong beside one another?

Use separate columns for answers naming the company, answers recommending it, cited sources, observed referral visits, buyer self-reports and qualified opportunities. Each needs its own numerator, base, period and evidence. A summary can connect them as a sequence of questions without claiming that every step caused the next.

<span id="7-de-identification-and-privacy"></span>
## What information belongs in a public report?

Publish reviewed aggregates and authorised evidence. Customer identities, private deal details and unreviewed free text do not belong in a public source report. Share only what is necessary for the stated purpose and agreed permissions. A buyer can assess visibility from public product facts without submitting customer records. Read our [security guide](https://broadcastwell.com/security) and [procurement guide](https://broadcastwell.com/procurement).

<span id="8-the-proof-page-and-report"></span>
## How can a buyer verify the conclusion?

Ask for the definition, base and dated evidence behind each measure. Inspect a complete answer for a visibility claim and the agreed business record for a commercial outcome. Read exclusions and unknown sources. A clear report states what the evidence establishes and lets the reader check the claim at the appropriate level.
