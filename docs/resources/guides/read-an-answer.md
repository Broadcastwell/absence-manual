<p><strong>Decision:</strong> Decide what one answer supports before recommending work.</p>
<p>Original Broadcastwell proposed operating method. Synthetic examples illustrate the method; they are not observed client outcomes.</p>
<h2>A screenshot is a starting point</h2>
<p>An answer is evidence of what appeared in that observation. Its usefulness depends on whether another reviewer can understand the question, inspect the permitted receipt and reproduce the classification. Before turning a screenshot into a business conclusion, name the buyer decision it was meant to investigate. A question about a small laboratory buying formulation software is different from a broad enterprise shortlist.</p>
<h2>Keep four things apart</h2>
<p>Record the answer text, the companies named, the returned source links and your interpretation in separate fields. A mention can be a passing reference or a warning rather than a recommendation. A linked source can contain a claim the answer misreads. Read the relevant source before treating it as factual support. A missing link does not reveal everything the system used.</p>
<h2>A worked example</h2>
<p>In the synthetic receipt DEMO-RECEIPT-001, the fictional response says: “Northstar Lab has a connector-based export; verify edition requirements.” That supports a present mention in this completed, reviewed fixture. It does not support “Northstar is the preferred vendor.” The independent fictional product sheet says the connector is third-party and requires Team edition. Comparing that sheet with a draft that says “native on every plan” reveals a concrete wording conflict.</p>
<h2>Keep the denominator honest</h2>
<p>The sample Audit has six planned fixture slots: three completed, one failed, one untested and one uncertain. Only two completed records have a reviewed mention classification: one present and one absent. The third completed record awaits review. Do not write “absent in five of six.” Repeated runs may share a question, source context or system behavior; their count is not a count of independent buyers.</p>
<h2>Identify the surface you used</h2>
<p>Keep engine, interface, provider, locale, timestamp and method version. If the interface is a local fixture, say so. If it is a provider API, name that interface; do not label it as a consumer-app test. Variability across systems is a reason to retain the receipt, not to choose only the most favorable answer.</p>
<h2>Make a decision</h2>
<p>The example supports reviewing the integration copy against the approved product facts. It does not show why any real engine included or omitted a company, or whether revenue changed. Complete the worksheet for one receipt. If the source or review is missing, leave the finding open. Then use the receipt demonstration to check your classification.</p>
