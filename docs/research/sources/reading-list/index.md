# A reading list for B2B marketers: which pages deserve your time?

**The 21 supplied files include repeated versions, edited volumes and practitioner guides; they are not 21 independent experiments (Broadcastwell collection inventory, 2026).**

Start with a result you need to check, rather than a book you feel obliged to finish. **21 physical files** make up this reading collection. Three are snapshots of the same F1000Research article, while two edited volumes contain chapters and articles with different designs. A file count is useful for locating the evidence; it is a poor substitute for counting independent studies. [Collection identity inventory](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/research/data/source-collection-inventory.csv).

The list below is an editorial route through the supplied editions. Each row names exactly three **PDF page numbers**, counted from the first page of that file. They may differ from printed page labels. That distinction matters especially in the Springer proceedings and the Wiley book, where a short article or interview sits deep inside a much longer file.

## What should you read and what can you skip?

The minute ranges are our suggested time budgets for the three selected pages, including a brief check of the adjacent discussion. They are **not measured reading times**, completion promises, or estimates for reading the whole document. Use more time when you intend to reproduce a result. A recommendation to skip an inference does not mean skipping an inconvenient limitation.

## Which route answers your question?

For **how often optimization signals appear in search results**, begin with Lewandowski’s classifier and chart. Read the discussion of Wikipedia before quoting its first-position result. Then compare the design with the registered AI-citation study when using that later dataset. The Google paper measures retrieved pages, not sales, brand preference or the probability that editing a page will win a citation.

For **whether a metric is actually observed**, compare the Jordan questionnaire with the university reanalysis. The former asks respondents about constructs that include perceived rankings; the latter includes deposited website measurements and rank variables. Those are different evidence objects. Neither design lets a marketer assume that a proposed intervention will move a buyer’s search result by the same amount.

For **an operational history**, use Google’s guide and the Ledford interviews. The useful exercise is to separate durable ideas about helpful information from instructions tied to retired products. Whalen’s concern about a single ranking as a success measure has a different evidential status from a keyword-density prescription. Both occur in the same book. Their proximity does not give the prescription experimental support.

For **an AI-search research review**, start with the Springer chapter’s selection method, then consult our dated literature census. A taxonomy can help organize questions even when it supplies no new intervention data. Treat the newer conceptual frameworks similarly. Look for the dataset, comparison condition and outcome before repeating a numerical improvement.

## Why retain the weaker documents?

They make the source chain inspectable. Removing a weak paper from a reading list can conceal how a familiar statistic reached a marketing slide. Keeping it with a specific limitation lets readers compare the actual wording with the opened primary source. The Anttila voice-search example is useful precisely because the same number can change meaning when “answer” becomes “query.”

A reference anomaly or unfinished editorial line is a reason to check the record. It is not a basis for claiming an author’s intent. The [statistic-checking guide](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/research/evidence/spot-a-fake-statistic/) distinguishes a visible defect, an unrecoverable input and a disproved numerical assertion. Use those distinctions when describing a source to colleagues.

The [graded source table](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/research/evidence/graded-sources/) grades support for particular uses. It does not grade the personal standing of the authors, and a strong historical study does not automatically answer a current engine question. Our [unverified-number list](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/research/evidence/unverified-numbers/) records claims that still need a recoverable primary basis.

## What our own data shows

We have no measurement on this point: the effect of following this reading order on a reader’s outcomes. The collection inventory records the supplied files and their identities; it is not a systematic sample of everything published about search. Our literature census uses its own recorded query and screening scope. Those two counts should not be merged.

Our September Index records dated answers to B2B software questions. It supplies a separate empirical reference point for the source reviews. A paper’s age, citation count or presence in this collection does not show that it explains those answers. Read the relevant measured unit and limitation before making that connection.
