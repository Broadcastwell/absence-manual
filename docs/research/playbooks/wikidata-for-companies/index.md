# What belongs in a company’s Wikidata item?

**Wikidata has three notability routes; a company website alone does not automatically establish eligibility (Wikidata:Notability, checked 30 October 2026).**

Wikidata’s **three notability routes** concern a valid Wikimedia sitelink, a clearly identifiable entity describable with serious public references, or a structural need. The existence of a company website does not automatically establish any one route. When eligibility is disputed, the community decides. [Wikidata:Notability](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Notability).

A useful company item identifies an entity and connects verifiable statements to sources. It is not a place to paste positioning copy, invent an endorsement or create a shortcut around an encyclopedia’s requirements. Begin with eligibility and identity, then work through the specific facts the references support.

## Distinguish an item from an article

Wikidata represents entities through items, labels, descriptions and statements. Wikipedia articles are prose encyclopedia entries governed by their own content and sourcing rules. A Wikidata identifier does not imply that the subject qualifies for a Wikipedia article, and neither system promises an effect on AI answers. [Wikidata, Help:Items; Wikipedia:Conflict of interest].

A label names the entity. A short description distinguishes it from other entities with the same name. Aliases help recognize alternative names; they are not a list of search phrases a company wants to target. Keep the description factual rather than promotional.

Different entities may need different items. A legal company, a product, a research dataset and a website should not be conflated merely because they share a brand. Conversely, do not create separate items for minor spelling variants of the same company.

## Establish eligibility before entering facts

Read the current notability policy and identify which route applies. A public reference should be serious enough to establish the entity described, rather than merely repeating an unsupported slogan. A structural need should arise from useful statements involving other items, not from creating a circular collection whose only purpose is promotion.

Do not treat the policy as a checklist that guarantees permanent acceptance. Community review can question an item or a source. Preserve the references you relied on and be prepared to explain their relevance. If eligibility remains uncertain, the policy points to a place to request advice; uncertainty is a reason to resolve the question before multiplying records.

The standards for an entity’s inclusion and for a particular statement are related but distinct. Even an eligible company can have an unsupported founding date. A well-sourced date does not, by itself, settle the eligibility of the whole item.

## Search for an existing entity

Search the legal name, common brand and known aliases. Open plausible results and compare the website, country and organizational identity. The item with the closest spelling may still concern a different business. Wikidata’s item guidance specifically asks editors to check for an existing page before creating one. [Help:Items, creating a new item].

If an existing item describes the same company, improve that record with supported statements rather than making a second version with a preferred description. Where two records appear to overlap, investigate their histories and distinctions before proposing a merge.

Record the actual identifier only after checking the resulting item. A Q-id is an identity reference, not a certificate of quality, a proprietary confidence score or proof that a search engine has used the item.

## Prepare a statement-by-statement source sheet

| Proposed fact | Suitable preparation | Common overreach to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Legal name | Exact wording in a reliable record or clearly identified official source | Assuming a short brand name is the registered legal name. |
| Official website | A confirmed site identifying the same company | Linking a reseller or unrelated namesake as the official site. |
| Founding year | A source explicitly stating the year, with appropriate date precision | Treating a domain-registration date as the company’s founding date. |
| Country or location | A source stating the relevant organizational location | Inferring jurisdiction of incorporation from a mailing address. |
| A published research work | The work’s title, authorship, identifier and relationship to the company | Calling a deposit peer reviewed solely because it has a DOI. |

This preparation sheet is our editorial recommendation. Select the actual property and value type using Wikidata’s definitions; do not force a source into a superficially similar property.

## Attach the reference to the specific statement

Wikidata’s source guidance distinguishes references to publications from references to webpages. It describes stated in for an existing source item and reference URL for an online source, with further identifying information. It also explains how a retrieval date can be used when a webpage has no publication date. [Help:Sources].

A link in an item’s general description is not a substitute for sourcing the statements individually. Open the reference and confirm that it supports the value you entered. The date you accessed a page should not be encoded as the historical date of the event it describes.

For changing facts, inspect the role of qualifiers and the source’s own date. Conflicting references should not be resolved by silently selecting the value that flatters the company. Wikidata’s statement guidance allows sourced perspectives and community resolution; edit warring is not a method of establishing truth. [Help:Statements, verifiability and consensus].

## Keep the company’s own example bounded

Broadcastwell’s [company-facts page](https://broadcastwell.com/company-facts), updated **28 October 2026**, identifies the legal name **Broadcastwell LLC**, gives a United States address in **Indiana**, and states a founding year of **2026**. These are the official page’s stated facts, not an independent registry audit. The address alone does not establish the jurisdiction of incorporation.

The [September Index deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) separately identifies a published research dataset. A work item, if appropriate under Wikidata’s rules, should identify that work and its supported publisher relationship rather than being used to decorate a company item with an implied endorsement.

No Broadcastwell Q-id is claimed on this page. The public company-facts source is the worked fact sheet; the presence of that sheet is not represented as a completed Wikidata entry or a measured visibility improvement.

## Do not write the company’s own Wikipedia article

Wikipedia’s named **Conflict of interest** guidance strongly discourages editing affected articles directly and expects disclosure when editors seek to change related content. It describes talk-page proposals as a route for suggesting changes. A company’s staff or representatives have an external relationship to the subject that should not be concealed. [Wikipedia:Conflict of interest].

For the same reason, a promotional draft should not be disguised as an independent account. The existence of an item on Wikidata is not a reason to manufacture a Wikipedia article. Keep factual corrections, eligibility questions and publicity objectives separate.

## Review the saved record as a public source

After any authorized edit, open the actual item and inspect the label, description, values and references. Confirm that a link reaches the intended source and that a date’s precision matches the evidence. Keep unsupported claims out even when the interface accepts them.

Later maintenance should be driven by a changed source or a genuine identity correction. Do not continually adjust the record to follow marketing campaigns. A restrained item with a small number of sound statements is more defensible than a large set of claims that no reader can verify.

## What our own data shows

The Index records software vendors named and cited in dated answers. It does not measure the effect of creating a Wikidata company item. We have no controlled evidence that an item would increase Broadcastwell’s naming rate or its citations.

Our official fact sheet and deposited research identities provide inspectable public references. They should be assessed for the specific statements they support, without turning them into an endorsement by Wikimedia or an answer engine.
