# Google itself asks for original research

**Five versioned research deposits establish publication, not a visibility gain (Broadcastwell, 2026, Zenodo record metadata; Google, 2010, p. 14).**

Google's **2010** SEO Starter Guide explicitly suggested that a publisher could “write an original piece of research.” The surrounding passage concerns compelling, useful content that readers may recommend. It does not attach a measured ranking gain to that action. [Google, 2010, printed p. 14](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/www.google.com/en//webmasters/docs/search-engine-optimization-starter-guide.pdf).

The sentence has aged better than many product-specific instructions in the same guide. Current Search Central guidance still asks publishers to contribute original information and substantial value. That continuity supports a publishing choice. It does not turn the choice into a guaranteed route into an AI answer.

You can produce a useful measurement that receives little attention. A citation can be misleading evidence of quality: a poorly supported page may still receive one, leaving you to inspect the underlying method yourself. Publication quality and observed discovery are related questions worth investigating; they are not interchangeable outcomes.

## Read the recommendation in its original setting

The old guide discusses knowing what readers want and writing material they can understand. Research appears among examples of content that can offer something new. The context includes users discovering and recommending content through ordinary channels. It is not a technical specification for an engine's private selection process.

This matters because a short quotation can acquire a much stronger meaning when it moves into a marketing presentation. “Google recommends original research” is supported by the primary guide. “Google gives original research a fixed ranking bonus” is not established by that passage. A citation should not conceal the difference.

The edition also has a date. We use it for the historical recommendation, while checking present operational advice against current documentation. A publishing principle can remain useful after named services and interfaces change. The enduring idea does not validate every implementation detail in an old manual.

## Original does not automatically mean informative

A new dataset is not useful merely because nobody has published those rows before. A buyer needs to know what the observations describe, how the outcome is defined and what cannot be inferred. A tally with no eligible base may be original and still leave the central question unanswered.

Current [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks about completeness, accuracy, clear sourcing and meaningful value beyond copying other pages. It also warns against exaggerated headings and changing dates merely to create the appearance of freshness. We use those as editorial questions, without claiming to know their algorithmic weights.

For a software company, a useful contribution might document a buyer problem, compare public specifications carefully or release observations with a stable definition. The contribution should survive a reader arriving directly, without a search engine in between. If it exists only to repeat a fashionable visibility claim, the reader still has to go elsewhere for the answer.

## What does the publication record establish?

Our versioned records make public observations inspectable. Their existence is evidence of publication and data availability. It is not, by itself, evidence of their effect on search visibility.

## Publication, use and citation need separate receipts

A repository view indicates a repository event under that service's counting rules. A download indicates a different event. Neither is a scholarly citation, a source link in an AI answer or a visit from a qualified buyer. We do not rename those metrics to make the publication record sound more consequential.

Likewise, a DOI gives a reader a persistent identifier and a route to a specific record. It does not certify peer review or guarantee inclusion in a search index. A citation to a DOI can be useful because its destination is identifiable, while the method behind the DOI still requires scrutiny.

We have not established a current citation count for these records from an appropriate citation source in this review. An unavailable count should not be presented as zero. Nor does an isolated answer citing a Broadcastwell page identify the causal contribution of research to that answer: other properties of the page and the surrounding public record may matter.

The historical self-audit sometimes cited in discussions of our research requires its original dated answer receipt before we repeat it as evidence. This page does not present an unverified recollection as a measured visibility result. The observable record above remains publication, with a clear boundary around impact.

## What would make the worked record stronger?

For an AI citation claim, the relevant receipt would retain the actual answer, its date, question, engine and explicit cited destination. The source would need to support the statement attributed to it. A screenshot of a brand name without its question or source context would answer a narrower question.

For a claim about an increase, the comparison would need compatible observations before and after publication. The conditions and outcome definition must remain visible. A causal claim would need a design that addresses competing explanations, rather than simply finding a citation sometime after a report appeared.

For a commercial claim, the evidence would have to connect discovery with an appropriate business outcome. A downloaded report and a signed customer agreement are different events. We cannot connect them here.

## Why publish when the visibility effect is unknown?

Original research can be worth publishing because it resolves a question someone actually has. A source review that catches a denominator error gives a reader a more accurate statement. With a dataset that identifies its exclusions and preserves its eligible base, another reader can inspect the count rather than relying on the publisher’s chosen headline. Those are concrete benefits to the reader even when a visibility effect has not been measured.

There is a trade-off. Maintaining a useful record takes attention, and the result can be inconvenient for the publisher. A correction may make a headline less striking. A null or unresolved finding may remove the promised story. The alternative is to publish only the observations that make the publisher look good, which leaves readers unable to evaluate the missing cases.

Our standard therefore asks a practical question before a promotional one: can another person use this record without relying on our assurance? The next citation, if it appears, will still need its own dated receipt. The recommendation in Google's old guide does not supply that receipt in advance.

## What our own data shows

We have no measurement on this point: the public deposit history does not establish an effect of original research on Broadcastwell's rankings, AI-answer citations or commercial outcomes. The linked Zenodo records establish publication and identifiable versions. Their dates and identifiers must not be repurposed as evidence of a visibility gain.
