# What the research says about search and AI answers

**Of 1,974 Wikipedia results, 27% appeared first; that is not Wikipedia’s share of all first positions (Lewandowski et al., 2021, §5).**

The claim that Wikipedia occupied roughly a quarter of all first positions is not what the cited paper measured. Lewandowski and colleagues described the positions occupied by Wikipedia's own results. That small change in wording changes the population. [Lewandowski et al., 2021, §5](https://searchstudies.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/3447535.3462479.pdf).

Jill Whalen's historical warning about ranking measurement, “an impossible measurement,” is a useful companion to these tables. It is attributed practitioner testimony, not a new experimental finding. [Ledford, 2009, printed p. 448].

We started this section by reading a supplied collection of search research, textbooks, operator guidance and recent writing about AI answers. The collection contains **21 PDF documents**, including an edited article collection and a large conference volume; it is not a set of independent experiments of equal quality. The [source ledger](evidence/graded-sources/) separates the articles and editions that those containers hold. The [evidence standard](standard/) explains what each grade permits.

You can use these pages to find a defensible claim, inspect a familiar statistic or decide what to measure next. Google search positions, scholarly relevance ordering and vendors named in AI answers are different outcomes. We place their findings beside each other where they illuminate a practical question, while retaining their original units.

## What is each study’s current status?

**Study 1, optimization signals on cited pages:** the existing plan is public at [OSF dvyfu](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/DVYFU), with platform registration timestamp 30 October 2026 at 01:06:12 UTC. No result is presented here. The study concerns observable signals and citation samples; those signals cannot establish why an engine selected a page.

**Study 2, publishers cited for B2B software questions:** the plan is registered at [OSF vczkt](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/VCZKT), 30 October 2026 at 01:32:03 UTC. Its registered secondary analysis concerns the explicit citation arrays in the September archive. The [results and reproducibility files](studies/who-ai-engines-cite/) preserve the units, repeated-link handling, control questions, uncertain publisher classes and post-analysis corrections.

**Study 3, persistence of absence:** the prospective plan is registered at [OSF mkj9v](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/MKJ9V), 30 October 2026 at 01:12:44 UTC. Its persistence analysis requires a further eligible release under comparable conditions. The [published waiting protocol](studies/does-absence-persist/) keeps the existing August and September standings as dated observations; they are not a measured transition.

Registration preserves a plan. It does not mean a result exists or that a study has passed peer review. Completed descriptive tables from an existing release are identified separately from these registered studies.

Read the original-study reviews of [Lewandowski and colleagues](sources/lewandowski-2021/) and [Rovira and colleagues](sources/rovira-2019/), including the latter’s deposited calculation discrepancy. The [Index against the literature](insights/index-against-the-literature/) compares their questions without treating different outcomes as interchangeable. The [crawler log](studies/ai-crawler-log/) is a separate, bounded public-request record; it does not establish citation.

## Take a number with its boundary

The [quotable numbers](evidence/quotable-numbers/) page gives the observation, source location and wording that can travel together. Its companion, [numbers we could not verify](evidence/unverified-numbers/), retains claims whose sources are missing, whose units changed in retelling or whose original papers do not support the advertised inference.

Some rejected claims have useful ancestors. A historical Google Home study measured the length of spoken answers, for example; it did not establish the length of the user's voice query. Recovering the original does not make its observation current. Both the date and the measured object still matter.

The Absence Manual supplies the longer explanation of [measurement noise](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/measurement-noise/) and [engine divergence](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/engine-divergence/). The [answer library](https://docs.broadcastwell.com/answers/) addresses individual buyer questions. This section supplies the source reviews, definitions and evidence behind those practical decisions.

One inconvenience remains deliberate. When a source's table and prose disagree, the uncertainty stays visible. A useful page may end with an unresolved question, particularly when the alternative would be a neat claim that the underlying record cannot carry.

## What our own data shows

The September release is a fixed set of captured answers, not a survey of every buyer or every possible question. Its [versioned primary record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) and the derived tables linked from the individual insight pages keep the observation base visible. We have no measurement on this point: publishing this research section itself has not yet been shown to improve Broadcastwell's AI citation or search traffic.
