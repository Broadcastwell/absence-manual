# Twenty-one source documents, graded by what they support

**Lewandowski’s classifier labels 29.0% of 256,853 sampled Google result URLs probably optimized, retaining uncertainty (Lewandowski et al., 2021, Figure 1).**

The supplied collection contains **21 physical PDF documents**, with duplicated editions and multi-article volumes. It does not contain 21 independent empirical studies. The inventory below counts files; the grading tables separate the works and uses inside them.

Our grades describe what a source can support in this section. They are not a ranking of authors, a declaration about an entire journal or an allegation of misconduct. You can find a useful historical quotation in a book whose unsourced traffic figures we would not repeat.

The [evidence standard](../../standard/) defines the scale. Grade A permits a scoped empirical result; B requires its material limit beside the claim; C is used for a documented evidence problem or descriptive framing; D is outside this section's substantive question. Neither A nor B is permission to make a causal claim from an observational design.

## Which conference chapters address these questions?

Ziakis and Vlachopoulou's AI-and-SEO review, DOI **10.1007/978-3-031-51038-0_43**, is C for outcome claims. It organizes a topic and describes a literature-selection process. A taxonomy and a review count are not experiments establishing that a tactic improves present-day AI citation.

Other potentially relevant case material concerns Meslem and Abbaci's traffic-channel comparison; Constantin and collaborators' translation examples; Constantin and Kavoura's museum websites; Mardache and Balasescu's student perceptions; a review-summary application in Mexico; European university chat facilities; and Xanthopoulou and colleagues' corporate-responsibility communication study. These are narrow contextual cases, not a body of evidence for B2B answer-engine effectiveness. Quantitative reuse requires the individual chapter's exact outcome, source date and limitations.

The corporate-responsibility case is **chapter 68**, DOI **10.1007/978-3-031-51038-0_68**. Its website-communication counts may be described as that small case study; they cannot establish an effect on AI answers. The primary identifier distinguishes it from similarly titled material elsewhere in the collection.

## A visible defect is a bounded finding

The LPU text says, “As of my knowledge cutoff in September 2021,” in its Core Web Vitals discussion, PDF p. 146. That sentence is a verifiable editorial artifact in the supplied text. It does not tell us who drafted the passage, how the book was produced or whether every instruction elsewhere is wrong.

The same restraint applies to a faulty reference or an untraceable pooled effect. We identify the defect and the claim it prevents us from accepting. We do not infer fraud, an entire journal's quality or a literature-wide failure from a selected reading collection.

The awkward cases remain useful. A survey may reveal how respondents understand SEO while failing to establish ranking effects. A practitioner may identify a practical measurement problem before a later dataset can quantify it. The grade controls the sentence we write, and the precise use can change when better primary evidence becomes available.

## What our own data shows

We have no measurement on this point: this selected collection is not a representative sample of the SEO or GEO literature, and its grade distribution cannot estimate the quality of the field. The physical-document count comes from the supplied collection inventory; article and edition distinctions above prevent duplicate files from being counted as independent findings.
