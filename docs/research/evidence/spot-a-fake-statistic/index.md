# How can you check an SEO statistic before you cite it?

**The reported 29 words describe answers, not queries; changing that noun changes the finding (Dean, 2018; Anttila, 2021, p. 9).**

**29 words** became a different statistic when a secondary account described it as voice-search length and used it to discuss longer queries. The opened primary study describes the average length of spoken answers in its Google Home sample. [Anttila, 2021, p. 9; Dean, 2018, answer-length section](https://backlinko.com/voice-search-seo-study).

The number survived. The object being measured did not. Before you cite an impressive SEO or GEO figure, inspect the noun, the base, the comparison and the source identity. A real paper can be misquoted, a numerical table can support less than its discussion claims, and a missing source is different from a disproved result.

## Has the measured object changed in retelling?

| Check | Opened source and exact location | Short excerpt or numerical detail | What the record supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer length versus query length | Anttila, 2021, p. 9; Dean, 2018, answer-length section | Anttila: “Voice search lengths are 29 words on average.” | Dean’s historical sample concerns 10,000 Google Home results and an average spoken-answer length. It does not establish an average query length or a required page length. |
| Included-study count | Usmany et al., 2024, printed p. 809, PDF p. 3 | Methods: “50 studies”; results: “10 relevant publications” | The method and results counts conflict. Table 2 labels rows P1 to P10 without mapping those labels to the included studies. |
| Correlation versus attributable percentage | Ahmad et al., version 2, Table 1, PDF p. 5 | “+0.854” and “Strong (85.4%)” appear in the same row | A Pearson correlation is not the percentage of brand positioning produced by SEO. The convenience-sampled questionnaire does not establish that causal interpretation. |
| Editorial completeness | Singh and Kesarwani, 2025, printed p. 4132, PDF p. 4 | “next point same instructions” | The fragment is an observable editorial defect. It does not establish who wrote the article or prove fraud. |
| Traceable reference identity | Ahmad et al., version 2, references 4, 7 and 11, PDF p. 7 | Different named journals reuse volume 38, issue 4, pages 567 to 584 | Repeated bibliographic coordinates require an identity check. Repetition alone does not prove that every listed work is nonexistent. |

The table names the source of each defect. It does not turn a selected reading collection into an estimate of how often SEO publishing contains such problems. Excerpts are short and serve to locate the claim being assessed.

## Does the denominator match the sentence?

A figure about one selected group can sound much larger when its conditioning group disappears. Lewandowski and colleagues describe where Wikipedia’s own results appear: their **27%** first-position figure is conditional on **1,974 Wikipedia results**. It is not Wikipedia’s share of every first-position slot in the study. [Lewandowski et al., 2021, §5, PDF p. 7, printed p. 18].

That error needs no dishonest author. It can arise when a sentence is shortened for a slide or repeated from a secondary summary. Restoring the denominator is often more useful than arguing about whether the headline sounds plausible.

Ask what entered the denominator and what was excluded. A citation occurrence, a distinct URL, an answer, a vendor-answer pair and a company are different units. Repeated links and repeated answers create additional choices. A percentage without those choices is difficult to use responsibly.

For a reported share, request the integer count and eligible base where they exist. Do not manufacture an exact numerator from a rounded percentage. When the source does not give sufficient counts, the absence of a reconstructable Wilson interval should be stated rather than filled with invented precision.

## Can you connect a result row to its underlying study?

The Usmany paper reports a pooled **d = 1.049**, but its included-study count and anonymous result-row labels prevent a reader from reconstructing the claimed synthesis from the reported mapping. The numerical appearance of a meta-analysis does not remove that missing link. [Usmany et al., 2024, Tables 2 and 3, PDF p. 3].

A reader needs to know which studies produced the rows, what each measured, how outcomes were converted and how uncertainty was handled. Otherwise a pooled value may look like a common scale while combining uninspectable inputs. Recalculating a mean from a printed table would still not validate the provenance of those inputs.

The IJSREM framework gives another selection problem. It says **60 high-relevance sources** were shortlisted, while the supplied reference list contains **16 entries**. We can count those entries and locate the selection statement; we cannot recover the unlisted selection from that statement alone. [Raju et al., 2025, p. 5 and pp. 9 to 10].

Its own methods also state that no original empirical dataset was collected. That makes it unsuitable as evidence for a numerical citation uplift merely because it presents an organized framework. A conceptual paper can be useful as a framing while remaining untested as an intervention. [Same paper, §3.5, p. 5].

## Is the statistic the kind of number the prose claims?

Ahmad and colleagues’ Table 1 reports **r = 0.854** in a questionnaire study of **275 convenience-sampled entrepreneurs**. The value describes an association between defined survey variables. It is not an attributable share of business performance. [Version 2, methods and Table 1, PDF p. 5].

Even squaring a correlation would not establish a causal percentage in this design. It would produce another descriptive quantity under particular statistical assumptions. The central problem is not solved by replacing one percent sign with a different calculation while leaving the causal sentence intact.

There is a smaller arithmetic discrepancy in the same table: **0.823** appears beside **83.3%**. We preserve that mismatch rather than silently correcting the authors’ display. A transcription issue and an invalid interpretation are separate findings, and either can matter to the sentence you plan to cite. [Table 1].

For correlations, effect sizes and model fit statistics, ask what the scale represents. A familiar decimal is not automatically a success probability. Wilson intervals belong to suitable count-based shares, not to every number that can be multiplied by a hundred.

## Does an editorial fragment justify an accusation?

No. The IJRPR article’s instruction fragment is visible in the original. That warrants describing the defect and examining whether the comparative ROI claim has an auditable outcome dataset. It does not reveal the production history or the authors’ intentions. [Singh and Kesarwani, 2025, PDF p. 4].

Calling a statistic false, calling a method inadequate and accusing someone of fabrication are different claims. Each needs its own evidence. We withhold a performance claim when its support cannot be traced; we do not need an accusation to explain why the claim is unsuitable for a buyer-facing page.

The same restraint applies to references. In the F1000Research revision, several groups of references reuse journal coordinates, including repeated page ranges across different journal names. The exact entries can be inspected on p. 7. That is a reason to look for the named work in a publisher or repository, not permission to invent the result of a search that was never completed.

## Which version are you reading?

A revision can change both the text and the review record. The supplied F1000Research files include distinct displayed versions or review states of the same study. They do not become independent replications because there is more than one PDF. Use the version identifier when quoting a table or reviewer comment. [Ahmad et al., version 2 title page and DOI; collection identity inventory].

Our own reading can also be wrong. The **48.4, 27 and 31** values in Lewandowski’s surrounding discussion concern news-content shares. They are not alternative optimization-class shares contradicting Figure 1. We withdrew that proposed contradiction after opening the source. [Lewandowski et al., §4 and Figure 1].

That correction belongs in the record alongside the criticism of external claims. A checklist that only catches other people’s errors is a weak safeguard against repeating its own.

## What should accompany the sentence you intend to use?

Keep the exact observation, its date, its eligible base and a source location together. If the paper’s prose conflicts with a table or deposited calculation, put the conflict beside the disputed number. If the original is unavailable, label the claim unverified and explain which part of the chain remains missing.

For fictional Cedarline Software, a board-slide claim about being cited should identify the captured questions, engines, dates and explicit source links. A sentence about customer outcomes would need different evidence. The apparent neatness of one percentage cannot supply the missing measurement.

The [unverified-numbers ledger](../unverified-numbers/) keeps the unresolved claims and the corrections separate. A later primary source may resolve a gap. Until then, the sentence can remain narrower than the headline.

## What our own data shows

In September 2026, **39 of 2,095 valid target answers** have no explicit cited URL, **1.86%**, descriptive **95% Wilson interval 1.36% to 2.53%**. [Release 2026-09](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695); [answer-citation bases](../../data/answer-citation-bases.csv).

That wording reports the captured citation field. It does not claim that the answer had no underlying information source, that every system always supplies citations, or that the percentage represents all buyer searches. The short sentence still needs its observable unit and its boundary.
