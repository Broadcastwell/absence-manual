# What does our agency training cover?

Our agency training covers four things an agency team needs before it puts our evidence in front of a client: reading an AI answer without overclaiming, approving ten buyer questions, checking a delivered report against its order, and handing the report to the client. It is free, takes about three and a half hours, and uses fictional practice files only.

Updated 9 November 2026.

The path strings together material we already publish. The lessons are open modules of [Broadcastwell Launchpad](launchpad.md); the preparation steps come from our agency onboarding guide, reviewed 6 November 2026; the practice report is the fictional agency workspace. This page puts them in order, adds exercises with answers, and says which parts are the agency's job and which are ours.

## What the report your team will check contains

The Agency Deadline Audit is $490 per client report. Per our [for agencies page](https://broadcastwell.com/for-agencies), it covers ten agreed buyer questions, five engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode) and three repeats: 150 planned answers, with valid answers and exclusions reported separately. The report carries your agency's name and color, three prioritized fixes and one paste-ready change of 250 words or fewer. Your agency implements the change; full-page deployment and ongoing work are not included.

Three terms matter in training because staff repeat them to clients:

- **The clock.** Findings arrive within 48 hours of recorded scope confirmation. The hours are elapsed hours, so a weekend counts.
- **The refund.** The report is refundable in full on request within 30 days of delivery.
- **The relationship.** We never contact your client during the work or for twelve months after.

Our [operating framework](operating-framework.md) quotes every delivery clock and refund term across the ladder, and our [service model](service-model.md) shows which workstreams this report includes.

## Exercises

Each exercise uses the fictional company Kalvenor Systems. Nothing in them is a client result.

**1. The deadline.** Your reviewer records scope confirmation at 14:00 UTC on Friday 6 November 2026. When are findings due?

*Answer:* 14:00 UTC on Sunday 8 November 2026. The clock counts elapsed hours from the recorded confirmation, so the weekend is included, and the account's recorded time is the source, not the email that announced it.

**2. The question set.** A colleague proposes "Is Kalvenor good for field service teams?" as one of the ten questions. Keep it or replace it?

*Answer:* replace it for a shortlist test. A question that names the brand tests a different task from an unprompted shortlist. Use a buyer task instead, for example which field service software supports offline technician checklists, and keep wording, market and buyer consistent across all ten.

**3. The review.** A delivered fictional report shows "named in 9 of 15" for one question but its evidence file lists 14 valid answers and one failed capture. Accept it?

*Answer:* not yet. The base should be the valid answers, 14, with the failed capture listed separately and visible as a failure. Ask through the order's support route before forwarding the report. Our page on [valid measurement](valid-measurement.md) explains why a failed answer is not a miss.

## Checks before your team forwards a report

These six checks come from step 3 of the onboarding guide:

1. **Identity.** The agency cover and client name match the approved brief.
2. **Scope.** The ten questions, five engines and three repeats match the confirmed order.
3. **Evidence.** Each result has a date, question, engine and answer receipt; valid and failed answers are separated, with the denominator shown.
4. **Action.** Three fixes are prioritized, each with its source and reason.
5. **Copy.** The paste-ready change is 250 words or fewer and uses facts the client can support.
6. **Handoff.** The receiving team can open the report and its evidence files.

Record the order reference, delivery time, report version, reviewer and any open correction together. A later live answer is a new observation, not a promise that an engine will repeat the earlier result.
