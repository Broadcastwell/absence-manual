## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Look past the tie. Minuttia's Price cell is Partial, while Go Fish Digital's is No. The source explains that Minuttia publishes a typical minimum engagement in its machine-readable company information, labelled as a directory-listing figure. That is different from a service-page rate card, so do not turn the Partial into a complete commercial offer.

Both Method cells are Partial. The Minuttia entry nevertheless links a research methodology with sampling and limitations, while the criterion remains unmet in full. Read that document for the particular evidence you need instead of assuming Partial means the same missing detail at both agencies.

For Open data, this scorecard asks for open data published with a DOI. Minuttia's entry describes downloadable aggregate research data but explicitly says the study carries no DOI. That is the source's concrete limitation behind this comparison, separate from the Method criterion about a published method and a complete question set.

For an agency choosing a research partner, the Machine readable column distinguishes Partial for Minuttia from No for Go Fish Digital. Open the linked company-information source and check the field you would need to reuse. Neither status authorises copying a competitor's claimed client results into your own proof.

The Refund column is No for both records. If that term matters to your decision, neither dated record supplies a qualifying published condition; it remains a specific question for the proposal, alongside the method and data you expect to receive.
