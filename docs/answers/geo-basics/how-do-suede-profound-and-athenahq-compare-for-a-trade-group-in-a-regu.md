# How do Suede, Profound and AthenaHQ compare for a trade group in a regulated industry?

If your trade group needs issue-level reporting, Suede's official page explicitly names trade associations; the comparison with Profound and AthenaHQ still needs clear report objects. In our [22 September 2026 Index](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695), 1 of Claude's 30 CRM returns cites a HubSpot blog URL whose path names Microsoft Dynamics and Zoho: the publisher and the named products are different identities.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [September Index method and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) count one HubSpot-root citation among thirty Claude CRM answers. The recorded URL is `blog.hubspot.com/sales/microsoft-dynamics-vs-zoho`; the root-domain count retains its blog host. This B2B software citation example does not test trade-association fit, supplier performance or regulatory compliance.

**Reported.** Suede's Public Affairs page, read on 30 October 2026, names trade associations, and its regulated-industry page names sectors including energy, healthcare and financial services. Profound's AEI guide, read on 29 October, distinguishes brand visibility, citations and FactCheck. Its [FactCheck FAQ](https://www.tryprofound.com/features/answer-engine-insights/factcheck) lists Enterprise customers for that feature. AthenaHQ's FAQ lists brand mention, citation, recommendation, sentiment and content-gap fields.

**Reasoned.** A trade group's own domain, the member companies discussed in its material and an issue being described are different potential report subjects. The actual cited-publisher example clarifies that distinction; the official audience descriptions supply the provider-scope comparison.

## What this means for you

Keep the publisher visible. Our recorded HubSpot-owned URL contains other product names in its path. If your association publishes material about member companies, an association-domain citation count would identify the publishing site; it would not decide which member company an answer recommends. That is the reporting-object question the example makes concrete.

Suede supplies an explicit association audience in the inspected public-affairs FAQ. Its regulated-industry description then names company facilities and public-information records, with different question personas and approval before material ships. For a trade group evaluating an issue-information workflow, those are the specific documented subjects and handoff, rather than a measured advantage over the other suppliers.

Profound's AEI guide describes a citation as a reference to a resource and FactCheck as a check of specific claims against a source of truth. AthenaHQ's FAQ describes brand metrics and content gaps where answer engines misunderstand or lack knowledge about a brand.
