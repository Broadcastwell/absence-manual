# How do Suede and AthenaHQ compare for tracking citations across models?

Suede describes response-and-source drill-down, while AthenaHQ-supported [The Answer Signal](https://theanswersignal.com/methodology) defines citation share through eligible answers and normalized root domains. Our [Index](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) recorded a HubSpot-domain citation in 1 of 30 Claude CRM answers from 22 September 2026; the retained URL is on blog.hubspot.com.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [September Index method and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) use ten CRM questions and three Claude returns per question. One of those 30 answers contains a HubSpot-domain URL, counting the domain at most once per answer and retaining subdomains under the same root. We did not run either provider's platform, compare collection completeness or establish account entitlements.

**Reported.** Suede's official Platform and About pages were read on 30 October 2026. They describe persona-specific prompting, captured answers, stance and sentiment, with drill-down into the captured response and cited sources. The Answer Signal's method, version 1.0 of August 2026 and read on 29 October, is supported by AthenaHQ. It defines citation share as eligible answers citing a normalized root domain at least once; repeated links within an answer count once.

**Reasoned.** Our blog.hubspot.com example shows why a root-domain rule matters. Splitting the blog host from HubSpot's root would assign the same retained source to a different website bucket, rather than changing what Claude actually returned.

## What this means for you

Keep the subdomain. The one HubSpot-domain reference in our Claude panel has the path `/sales/microsoft-dynamics-vs-zoho`. It is a cited blog page, rather than one of the main-domain product URLs recorded elsewhere in the Index. If your comparison concerns a company's cited website, the domain-normalization rule determines where that observation belongs.

Suede's reported workflow starts with persona-specific questions and the captured response. Its platform page says readers can inspect the answer's cited sources along with stance and sentiment. Those are separately named fields: a cited-page observation is not a sentiment label. The About page places this workflow in institutional communications, including public affairs, advocacy, corporations and regulated industries.

The Athena-supported source is a research method, rather than a specification of every AthenaHQ account feature. It identifies eligible cohorts, excludes flagged responses and deleted records unless a study says otherwise, and treats repeated domain links within one answer as one citation observation. Its stated longitudinal comparisons use like-for-like cohorts.
