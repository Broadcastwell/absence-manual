# What does Blue Yonder publish about LTL freight shipping?

On 22 September 2026, our [transportation panel](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) named Blue Yonder in six of thirty ChatGPT answers. Blue Yonder’s [Transportation Execution description](https://blueyonder.com/solutions/transportation-management/transportation-execution) includes LTL among its published modes. The particular LTL function comes from that supplier page; our six observations record software names.

## What we measured

**Reported.** Blue Yonder’s official description, read on 30 October 2026, lists inbound and outbound transport across parcel, LTL, TL, rail, ocean, and private and dedicated fleets. It describes routing, scheduling and constraint logic continuing as shipments and loads are built, modified, tendered and executed. The page also names interoperability with Blue Yonder Network, including digital tenders and transactions.

**Measured.** The [Index release and method](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) contain ten fixed transportation questions with three ChatGPT returns each. Blue Yonder was named in six of those thirty answers. Across all five engines, its name appeared in 23 of 150 valid answers and its own domain was cited in eight. The capture did not test an LTL shipment, carrier coverage, rates or software implementation.

**Reasoned.** A transportation-software name in an answer and the supplier’s stated LTL function describe different evidence. Read the product description for the published operating scope and the released question record for the naming observation.

## What this means for you

Inspect the mode list. The supplier places LTL within a multi-mode execution description, alongside the other named transport modes. Its page discusses the relationship between planning and subsequent shipment changes. A question about tendering an LTL load therefore belongs with the execution functions, while a request for a freight price needs a separate quotation.

Keep the buyer context attached. All six ChatGPT names came from two questions: three returns about a large enterprise shipper and three about alternatives to MercuryGate. The other eight transportation questions supplied no ChatGPT naming observation for Blue Yonder. None of these ten question titles asks specifically for an LTL service. Their retained wording explains why the six-of-thirty result cannot be relabelled as six successful LTL recommendations.
