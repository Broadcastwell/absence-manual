# How might AI search engines change the web?

AI search can put a web page inside a composed answer as a supporting source, without selecting its publisher as a supplier. In our [18 to 23 July 2026 Volume II study](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21586091), naming and own-domain citation had a Pearson correlation of 0.512 across 85 software-company profiles. That observed separation is a firmer starting point than a prediction that websites will disappear.

Observed answers on stated dates, not a ranking.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [Volume II study method and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21586091) retain ten answers per company in a one-engine business-to-business (B2B) software sample. Each company's number of namings is paired with its own-domain citation count. Recalculating Pearson's coefficient from those eighty-five paired rows reproduces the published rounded value, 0.512. The Volume II source does not name the engine. Its one-engine, one-run result cannot establish a cross-engine result or a future change.

**Reported.** Google's [AI-features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), read on 29 October 2026, describes related searches across subtopics and supporting pages in a generated response. OpenAI's [Search help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt), read that day, separately describes rewriting a question into targeted queries sent to search providers.

**Reasoned.** These reported retrieval steps give publishers more than one possible role: a page can supply supporting information even when its author is not the business selected in the answer. The measured relationship records that the two reporting units differ; it does not reveal every retrieval step.

## What this means for you

Read the supporting link. Google describes Mode as useful for exploration and complex comparisons, with links to websites supporting its response. That presentation differs from a page serving only as a destination in a list of results. For a publisher, an answer's quoted source and its named supplier are two different things to inspect.

Google also keeps Search controls attached to those pages. Its documentation ties supporting-link eligibility to indexing and snippet eligibility, and identifies Googlebot as the Search crawl control. It names nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex as controls on information shown. Those are documented publisher choices about access and presentation, rather than a promise about how much traffic follows.

OpenAI describes a different piece of the route: it may rewrite a question for a search partner, inspect the result and issue a narrower query. The retained help example moves from a broad drug-development question to a specific conference query. A page relevant to that narrower request can therefore be a supporting source even when its title does not repeat the user's initial question.
