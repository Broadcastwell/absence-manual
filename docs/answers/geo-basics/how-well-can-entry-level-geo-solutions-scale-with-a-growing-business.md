# How well can entry-level GEO solutions scale with a growing business?

If your business is adding brands or clients, an entry-level GEO tool scales only as far as its documented resources and reporting boundaries allow. Our September Index retained 2,095/2,100 scheduled answers from captures on 22 and 23 September 2026. You need room for the missing or excluded observations as well as the usable ones. Otterly's shared prompt pool illustrates a resource boundary; it is not a measured performance result.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [September Index method and release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) retain the answer records and exclusions behind that total. The scheduled design covered 14 software categories, ten buyer questions in each category, five engine surfaces and three repeats. Five targets were excluded before the original vendor-presence calculations.

The volume describes our observation workload, not an Otterly benchmark. We did not import this dataset into an entry-level subscription, test its speed or measure the cost of scaling an account.

**Reported.** Otterly's [multiple-brand help](https://help.otterly.ai/can-i-manage-multiple-brands-under-one-account), dated 5 August 2026 and read on 29 October, distinguishes competitors within a Brand Report, separate reports for your own brands, and separate Workspaces for distinct clients. Its [Workspace guide](https://help.otterly.ai/what-are-workspaces-and-how-can-they-help-you-stay-organized-across-clients), dated 27 February 2026, describes allocating prompts and GEO Audits across those Workspaces.

## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Start with what is growing. Adding a competitor beside your brand is different from taking on a new client. Otterly documents the former inside one Brand Report and the latter in a separate Workspace. A dashboard that compares competitors does not settle who may see a client's reports or how that client's allocated resources are tracked.

Read the resource rule separately. The August help describes a subscription's shared search-prompt pool and says reusing the same prompt across reports does not require buying it twice. That does not mean every new question fits within the existing allocation. If a new client brings different buyer questions, those questions still belong in the allocation you are sizing.

The Workspace count is another distinct limit. Otterly reports unlimited Workspaces on the named Standard and Premium plans, while the February guide also names Custom Enterprise for the allocation feature. Neither statement makes the prompt pool unlimited. Keep the relevant plan's current terms beside the requested number of clients and questions; a resource statement from one plan is not an entitlement on another.

Our retained-answer total shows why the export matters as volume grows. A report containing only usable observations would omit the excluded targets needed to reconstruct the original schedule. For a larger client set, preserve the question, engine, repeat and exclusion reason so someone can reconcile the work without treating every missing response as a missing vendor.

The February guide describes admins changing prompt and GEO Audit allocations and assigning people to Workspaces. Those are published administration functions, rather than proof that we tested account separation. For your next client, write down the required Workspace access and prompt allocation together; that is the particular boundary to check against the documented plan.
