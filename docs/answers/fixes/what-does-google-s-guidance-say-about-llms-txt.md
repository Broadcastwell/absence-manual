# What does Google's guidance say about llms.txt?

On 29 October 2026, Google’s inspected site-owner guidance said new AI text files were not needed to appear in its Search AI features. Our separate [agency publication record](https://broadcastwell.com/best-geo-agencies#minuttia) scored Minuttia 3/8, with Machine readable marked Partial; a published llms.txt file is not proof of Google Search adoption.

## What we measured

**Reported.** Google's [AI-feature guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says supporting pages must be indexed and eligible for a Search snippet, without additional AI-specific technical requirements. The inspected [llms.txt proposal](https://llmstxt.org/) describes a file for providing documentation and links to language models. **Reasoned.** A proposal's intended purpose does not establish what Google's Search systems require or use.

**Measured.** The [eight-field agency audit](https://broadcastwell.com/best-geo-agencies#scorecard) preserves the Minuttia record below. The original company-site read was on 15 September 2026. ## What this means for you

Keep the two claims separate. The [Minuttia file](https://minuttia.com/llms.txt), read on 29 October 2026, establishes that this named publisher exposes such a document. It cannot establish which crawler consulted it or whether it changed an answer. Machine readable Partial is also a broader publication judgment, not a Google compliance grade for the file.

Read the stated requirement. Google's inspected guide directs site owners to ordinary Search eligibility and says no new AI text file is necessary. A claim that the file is mandatory for Overview inclusion would contradict that guidance. A claim that someone published it for documentation purposes can be true without contradicting Google at all.
