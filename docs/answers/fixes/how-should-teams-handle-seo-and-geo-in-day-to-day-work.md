# How should teams handle SEO and GEO in day-to-day work?

Procore's pricing flags give a team a concrete daily task: check the disputed claim, assign the relevant page work and record what was actually completed. Wrong About You marked 2/57 Procore statements wrong. The study captured those answers on 22 September 2026; the vendor-page check followed on 8 October. You can use those records to specify work; both still have team review in progress, so neither is a completed repair.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [Wrong About You classification method](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23270124) splits the Procore statements into 38 true, two wrong, zero stale and 17 unverifiable. A statement is the unit here. It is not a buyer question, a visit or a unit of revenue.

The Claude flag says Procore lacks a pricing page. The saved vendor evidence contains that page. The Google AI Overview flag instead describes per-user pricing, while the checked Procore passage says adding users carries no charge. Each disputed statement appeared in a single recorded repeat of the small-general-contractor comparison question.

These classifications did not measure a team's productivity or the effect of changing a page. They identify particular statements requiring review, with capture and vendor-source dates kept separate.

## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Give the Claude flag to the person who owns the pricing-page evidence. The job is to verify the existing page and the assertion about its existence. Publishing a fixed rate card would answer a different question; the flag does not establish that Procore publishes a fixed price. Put the saved statement and the pricing-page receipt in the task before a writer starts.

Treat the Overview flag as a separate work order. Its subject is the cost of adding users, so the reviewer needs the source passage addressing users. A general description of the pricing page will not settle that claim. If the proposed change concerns an external comparison, record the exact passage that needs review rather than sending a broad request to improve GEO.

Keep the 17 unverifiable statements out of an automatic wrong-fact queue. Their evidence state is unresolved. Assigning them all as errors would inflate the team's correction backlog and encourage edits without a checked contradiction. The zero stale statements likewise describes this dated vendor cut; it is no promise that today's product information needs no maintenance.

Use separate completion fields for the source check and any page change. A reviewer can finish reading the vendor evidence while a proposed correction is still waiting. A saved, served page can later demonstrate a publication; it cannot demonstrate that an assistant has changed its answer. For these two Procore records, the published review state remains in progress.

The Claude record also names Projul's comparison page among its cited material. That gives the daily reviewer a specific comparison to read alongside Procore's pricing source. It does not prove the cited page caused Claude's assertion. Attach both receipts to the pricing-page task so the next person can judge the particular disagreement without reconstructing it from a visibility chart.
