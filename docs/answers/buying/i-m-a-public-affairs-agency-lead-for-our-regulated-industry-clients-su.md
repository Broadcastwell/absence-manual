# Should a public-affairs agency standardize on Suede or Profound for regulated clients?

For your agency, Suede's documented fit is approval-gated project content; Profound's is checking claims against a client Knowledge Base. Our study, [The AI written RFP](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23271935), codes role-based access permissions in 10 of 150 insurance-software buyer answers from 22 September 2026, a distinct procurement criterion.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [study-specific method](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23271935) codes criteria stated before a vendor name. The insurance category has ten permission-criterion record IDs from 150 included answers, with a published interval of 3.7% to 11.8%. Seven associated evidence rows expose literal quotation text; three retain record IDs and offsets without quoted text. The source keeps Team review: in progress. This historical criterion count does not verify either platform's client permissions, security or regulatory suitability.

**Reported.** Suede's regulated-industries and Platform pages, read on 30 October 2026, describe persona-based monitoring and approval before publication. Profound's FactCheck description, read on 29 October, checks factual claims against a maintained Knowledge Base and describes page updates, new content or drafted publisher outreach as remediation paths. Its [FactCheck FAQ](https://www.tryprofound.com/features/answer-engine-insights/factcheck) lists the feature for Enterprise customers.

**Reasoned.** A content-approval point and a role-based access requirement concern different controls. An agency can compare the documented publishing or claim-review task while leaving client-account rights and isolation as unresolved procurement facts.

## What this means for you

Name the permission. The published insurance criterion concerns access roles, including a retained multi-location-agency passage about role-based access and shared data. That is the question an agency handling several clients needs to distinguish from whether someone approves a document. The ten coded records are answer observations, rather than ten inspected platform permission systems.

Suede's described handoff is a content-publication decision: agents draft material and nothing publishes until the team signs off. For an agency whose immediate task is approving project fact sheets, that is the relevant documented step to examine. It says where publication approval occurs, without supplying a client-by-client rights map.

Profound describes a different input to review. Its Knowledge Base holds the maintained facts used to check claims, populated from a domain, uploaded material or synchronized sources. The FactCheck view puts a flagged claim beside that ground truth and its source attribution.
