# Is agent-to-agent commerce a viable business opportunity?

No published protocol alone establishes that agent-to-agent commerce is a viable business opportunity. Our [Agent Gap study](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23197560) reached 44 of 70 initial public demo-path tasks on 6 October 2026; reaching a handoff was not a purchase, and it supplied no evidence of willingness to pay.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [Agent Gap study design](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23197560) asks whether an assistant can complete specified tasks on software vendors' public sites. Its S3 task follows the demo path. Reaching that endpoint records a discoverable buying step, rather than an order, authorized charge or fulfilled service.

**Reported.** The [Agentic Commerce Protocol architecture](https://www.agenticcommerce.dev/docs/concepts/architecture), read on 30 October 2026, describes four parties: buyer, agent, seller and payment provider. The buyer reviews the checkout and authorizes payment. The agent orchestrates the session; the seller remains the merchant of record and handles inventory and fulfillment. Payment providers process charges and enforce the delegated allowance constraints.

**Reasoned.** This is a concrete transaction design, but its existence does not identify a paying customer for your proposed service. An assistant reaching a seller and a buyer authorizing a purchase occupy different points in the chain. Agent Gap observes the earlier public-site boundary, not the protocol's completed checkout.

## What this means for you

Locate the proposed service. If it helps an assistant discover a vendor's demo route, the relevant output is a reached route with the correct destination. If it operates a checkout, the offer must instead say which transaction responsibility it performs and who remains responsible for delivery. Calling both “agent commerce” conceals the event a customer would pay for.

Keep the buyer visible. The architecture assigns the human a review and authorization role; it does not describe two unconstrained bots deciding to spend independently. A business plan that assumes payment follows automatically from an agent's intent has skipped an explicit protocol step.

The protocol's payment boundary is specific: Delegate Payment carries allowance constraints for amount, currency, expiration and merchant. Its seller component calculates tax and shipping, processes payment and fulfills the order as merchant of record. A service built around that interface needs to identify which of those named responsibilities it performs. Agent Gap's Procore S3 record, by contrast, ends at a reached public demo path on 6 October 2026; its recorded fields are found, correct and reached, rather than an order or payment result.
