# How can I evaluate Hotwire GAIO.tech as an AI visibility product?

On 27 March 2025, Hotwire identified Spark as the later name for GAIO.tech; start your evaluation with that identity record and the product's stated scope. Then ask which answer events its reports count. Our 22 September 2026 Jobber example recorded 50 naming answers and 46 own-domain citation answers from a 150-answer field-service base, illustrating why the event definition belongs in your review.

## What we measured

**Reported.** Hotwire's [AI Lab announcement](https://www.hotwireglobal.com/news-room/hotwire-global-announces-ai-lab-consulting/), read on 29 October 2026, names Spark as the product formerly called GAIO.tech. It describes visibility in chatbot answers and the sources of that information. The same announcement calls Ignite an account-intelligence product, so do not treat that separate product description as Spark's specification.

**Measured.** The Jobber example is from the [September release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695). Ten field-service buyer questions were repeated three times per engine, with 150 retained answers across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode. The published naming interval is 26.3% to 41.2%; the own-domain citation interval is 23.8% to 38.5%, at 95% confidence.

This Index example does not measure GAIO.tech or Spark's accuracy.

## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Keep Ignite separate. Keep the old and new names together in your buying notes. A document headed GAIO.tech may explain the product's earlier positioning, while the 2025 announcement discusses Spark. Ask which description applies to the proposal you are evaluating rather than filling the gap from the other Hotwire product.

For the report sample, ask for one saved answer and the decision attached to it. The Jobber figures show the precise distinction to inspect: was the company named, was its own address cited, or was another publisher the source? A broad visibility label leaves those questions open.
