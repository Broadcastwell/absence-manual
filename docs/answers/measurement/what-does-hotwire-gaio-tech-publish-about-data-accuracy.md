# What does Hotwire GAIO.tech publish about data accuracy?

Hotwire's GAIO.tech launch announcement and its later Spark announcement describe answer visibility and source information; the two pages we read on 29 October 2026 do not supply a claim-by-claim accuracy result. Our own Wrong About You study classified 1,924 of 2,922 extracted statements as true, using answers from 22 September 2026 and 23 September 2026, with the vendors' published facts inspected on 8 October 2026. That is an example of a defined classification, not an accuracy score for Hotwire's product.

## What we measured

**Reported.** The [GAIO.tech launch page](https://www.hotwireglobal.com/news-room/global-ai-innovation-gaio/) describes learning what chatbots say about a brand and which sources inform the answers. Hotwire's [27 March 2025 announcement](https://www.hotwireglobal.com/news-room/hotwire-global-announces-ai-lab-consulting/) calls Spark the product formerly known as GAIO.tech. The accuracy limitation here applies to those two pages inspected on 29 October 2026, not to every document Hotwire may hold.

**Measured.** The [Wrong About You method](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23270124) classifies statements extracted from answers captured on 22 and 23 September 2026. Its reference check used vendors' public pages read on 8 October. The 2,922-statement base includes true, wrong, stale and unverifiable classes; team review of flagged statements is in progress.

Our statement classification cannot establish the accuracy of GAIO.tech or Spark's data.

## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Resolve the name first. The 2025 announcement gives you the link from GAIO.tech to Spark. It also describes Ignite as a separate account-intelligence product. Material explaining Ignite's data should not silently become the evidence for Spark's answer-monitoring accuracy.

Next, distinguish collecting an answer from judging its truth. Hotwire's two announcements describe visibility and sources. The Wrong About You example instead asks whether an extracted factual statement agrees with a dated vendor source. A provider can describe the first task without having published a result for the second on those pages.

For a Spark report sample, ask which accuracy question the number answers: faithful capture of the engine's text, correct recognition of the brand name, or truth of the statement itself. The 1,924 true classifications concern the last of those, within our specified sample. They are not a benchmark you can transfer to Hotwire's collection system.

Wrong About You left 880 of its 2,922 statements unverifiable against the saved vendor pages. That is why its 1,924 true statements should not be presented over a base that silently removes every unresolved case. For a Spark validation sample, ask whether an unsettled claim remains a visible class or disappears from the reported base.
