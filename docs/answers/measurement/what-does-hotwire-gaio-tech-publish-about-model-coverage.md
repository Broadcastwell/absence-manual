# What does Hotwire GAIO.tech publish about model coverage?

On 21 May 2024, Hotwire announced GAIO.tech with ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity among its examples, alongside Google's conversational assistant. Its later Spark announcement names ChatGPT and Copilot alongside that Google chatbot. Keep those dated labels when you assess coverage: our own 22 September 2026 construction panel named Procore in 23/30 ChatGPT answers. That engine-specific count illustrates an output record, not a coverage test of Hotwire's product.

## What we measured

**Reported.** Hotwire's [launch release](https://www.hotwireglobal.com/news-room/global-ai-innovation-gaio/) carries a 14 June 2024 page date and a 21 May 2024 announcement dateline. It describes brand mentions, prominent answers and cited sources across the named chatbots. The text uses examples; it does not provide a versioned, exhaustive model-coverage manifest.

The [27 March 2025 release](https://www.hotwireglobal.com/news-room/hotwire-global-announces-ai-lab-consulting/) identifies Spark as formerly GAIO.tech. It describes added agents and synthetic personas, and uses a different example list of chatbots. Both source pages were read on 29 October 2026. Neither read is a test of an authenticated Spark account.

**Measured.** The [September Index method](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) gives Procore 23 named answers from the 30 retained ChatGPT observations in construction project management. Claude named it in 21 of that engine's 30 observations. Each base comprises ten buyer questions with three repeats, captured on 22 September 2026. These are our recorded outputs, not data supplied by Hotwire.

## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Resolve the product identity before comparing engine lists. The 2025 release links the older GAIO.tech name to Spark and describes Ignite separately. Its account-intelligence claims cannot be treated as Spark's chatbot coverage. For a document still headed GAIO.tech, keep the original date and the later identity source together.

The Google chatbot entry in these releases is not a documented promise to collect Google AI Overviews or Google AI Mode. Those are separately labelled surfaces in our Index, and the two Hotwire announcements do not settle their inclusion. The same distinction applies to a chatbot name and an underlying model version: one label does not identify every configuration behind it.

Different examples do not prove removal. Claude and Perplexity appearing in the launch text but absent from the later example list is insufficient evidence that Spark stopped covering them. Conversely, Copilot's appearance in the 2025 text does not tell you the start date, sampling schedule or settings of any captured answer. The release provides product statements, not that operational history.

The Procore comparison shows why an engine field belongs in a sample export. Its ChatGPT and Claude counts differ even with the same scheduled question-and-repeat design. A pooled brand total would hide that distinction. To reconcile a claimed Spark result, the relevant evidence is the actual engine or surface label attached to each returned answer, with its collection date and base, rather than the aggregate logo list in a launch announcement.
