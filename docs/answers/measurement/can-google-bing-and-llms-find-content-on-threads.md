# Can Google, Bing and LLMs find content on Threads?

A public Threads address does not by itself prove that Google, Bing or an LLM returns its content. We found no recorded threads.net or threads.com citation host in [2,093 included Answer RFP responses](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23271935), collected over 22 September 2026 and the following day. This source result describes our software-buyer sample, not whether a particular Threads post is indexed.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [Answer RFP method and records](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23271935) preserve citation-URL fields for the included responses. We checked both Threads hostnames, counting each host at most once per answer. Thirty-eight records have empty URL lists. Team review of the study remains in progress; missing recorded URLs are not a complete account of every source an engine could access.

**Reported.** Meta’s [April 2025 web announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/new-features-threads-web-experience/), read on 30 October 2026 UTC, describes the move from threads.net to threads.com. Its [December 2023 EU update](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/07/introducing-threads-new-app-text-sharing/), read the same day, describes browsing without a profile in that region.

**Reasoned.** Those dated access statements and our recorded citations cannot establish today’s Google or Bing index status for an individual post, or whether it was used in model training.

## What this means for you

Keep both hostnames. Meta’s migration announcement makes a search of only threads.net incomplete for a source review spanning the change. The absence we report comes from checking the two named hosts in stored citation fields; it is not a test of every URL on either domain.

The EU announcement has a narrower meaning than unrestricted crawler access. It describes people browsing content and searching accounts without creating a profile, while preventing them from posting or interacting. That historical user experience does not supply an engine’s index entry for the particular post you care about.

Google’s [AI feature requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) separately require an indexed, snippet-eligible page for a supporting link. A Threads post being visible to a person therefore leaves a separate Google state to establish. Our sample also provides no Bing inspection receipt for that post, so a Google observation should not be presented as Bing evidence.
