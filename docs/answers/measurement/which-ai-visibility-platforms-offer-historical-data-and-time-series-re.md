# Which AI visibility platforms offer historical data and time-series reporting?

Otterly documents daily prompt monitoring and cumulative counts; Profound documents daily answer datasets and charts. For context, our [Volume III study](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21789120) found a mean vendor-set Jaccard overlap of 0.499 across 62 Google AI Overviews repeat pairs on 4 August 2026, making the underlying runs material to a trend.

## What we measured

**Reported.** Otterly's [5 October 2026 monitoring guide](https://help.otterly.ai/search-prompt-monitoring), read on 29 October, describes daily collection across engines available in an account. Its prompt table distinguishes cumulative brand mentions and cumulative domain citations from per-response measures, and offers response-by-response and engine-level detail.

Profound's [Answer Engine Insights documentation](https://help.tryprofound.com/articles/3443229936-answer-engine-insights-overview), also read on 29 October, describes prompts sent daily, an aggregated response dataset and charts with their own configuration. It distinguishes visibility from share of voice; those measures use different denominators even when displayed in the same reporting area.

**Measured.** The [Volume III study design](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21789120) compared the sets of named vendors in original and repeated Overview answers. Jaccard overlap divides the shared vendor set by the combined set. The published mean across 62 pairs is a similarity score, not 0.499 of 62 answers or a percentage change in a brand's visibility. That study does not establish either platform's retention period, backfill or historical accuracy.

## What this means for you

Separate totals from rates. In Otterly's documented columns, another collected run can increase a cumulative citation total without showing that your citation rate improved. Keep the response count and the engine filter beside the cumulative series before using it as evidence of a change.

For a Profound export, retain the stated metric and its base. The AEI guide defines visibility using responses that contain at least one brand, whereas its share-of-voice explanation uses brand mentions. Switching between those charts is a change of measure, not simply another view of an identical percentage.

The 62 repeat pairs explain why a history request needs the answer records as well as the chart. A changed set of vendor names can arise between repeats. To investigate one movement, keep the two responses with their prompt, engine and collection date; then identify the changed names. That supplies a checkable event instead of attributing every bend in the time series to work completed on your website.
