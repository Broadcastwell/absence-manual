# Which construction software does Claude recommend?

Buildertrend and Procore each appeared in 21 of 30 Claude construction-software answers captured on 22 September 2026. Their equal totals hide a useful distinction: the small-residential-builder question named Buildertrend in all three answers and Procore in none. The mid-sized-general-contractor question reversed those two counts. Read that buyer context before treating the shared total as a recommendation for your firm.

## What we measured

**Measured.** In the [September construction release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695), Claude answered ten fixed questions three times each. The 30 valid answers cover residential building, general contracting, subcontractors, capital projects and comparisons. All were captured on 22 September 2026; the release was published the next day.

Buildertrend's 21 matches correspond to 70.0 percent. A descriptive 95 percent Wilson interval runs from 52.1 to 83.3 percent. Generalising that range to all construction buyers would go beyond this fixed panel.

## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Locate your contracting context before assembling a product list. A residential builder's question, an owner's capital-project comparison and a subcontractor's question should remain separate records. Pooling them can conceal the very distinction you need to judge an answer.

For each candidate, identify the claim you would rely on in a buying discussion. If the answer describes estimating, scheduling or a project connection, ask for dated product evidence for that specific claim. A recorded appearance of Buildertrend or Procore does not establish the detail by itself.

If you publish construction-product information, explain the supported work and its boundary on the relevant page. Keep missing facts separate from a missing brand name. The first can be checked against a source; the second needs the actual question and captured answer before you can investigate it.
