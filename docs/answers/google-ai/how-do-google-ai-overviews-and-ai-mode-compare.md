# How do Google AI Overviews and AI Mode compare?

Google AI Overviews and AI Mode serve different Search experiences, and their answers can differ even for the same buyer question. On 22 September 2026, Procore was named in 0 of 3 Overview answers and 3 of 3 AI Mode answers to a question about software for a small residential builder. This compares one vendor's recorded presence, not which experience is better.

## What we measured

**Reported.** In its [AI-feature guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), read on 28 October 2026, Google describes Overviews as a quick explanation and starting point for further links. AI Mode supports deeper exploration, reasoning and comparisons. Google states that the experiences may use different models and techniques and that responses and links can vary.

**Measured.** The [September Index release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) held the construction category and question fixed while recording three scheduled runs for each engine. For Procore, Overviews' 0 of 3 has a descriptive 95% Wilson interval of 0.0% to 56.1%; AI Mode's 3 of 3 has an interval of 43.9% to 100.0%.

Buildertrend and Contractor Foreman were each named in all three answers on both experiences for this question. **Reasoned.** The Procore contrast therefore does not mean the two complete shortlists had no overlap. These small counts cannot establish a general engine ranking, a causal explanation for the difference or what either experience will produce next.

## What this means for you

Use separate columns for AI Overviews and AI Mode when a business needs to understand both. Preserve the same question, dates and run counts so the difference has a clear basis.

Compare the event you actually collected. A naming difference is useful for presence analysis; citation quality requires checking the links and their supporting passages. Do not borrow an all-engine citation total to fill an experience-specific gap.

For your own buyer research, read the answers themselves. A missing vendor name in one captured experience does not establish that its product is unsuitable, nor does presence in the other settle a purchase decision.
