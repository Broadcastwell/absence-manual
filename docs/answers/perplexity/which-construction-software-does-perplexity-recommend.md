# Which construction software does Perplexity recommend?

For a small residential builder, Perplexity's recorded names differed from its larger category totals. Procore appeared in 21 of 30 construction answers captured on 22 September 2026, but in none of the three small-residential-builder answers; Buildertrend, Contractor Foreman and JobTread appeared in all three. The counts answer which names appeared, without testing which product you should buy.

## What we measured

**Measured.** The [September Index](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907695) asked ten construction questions three times on Perplexity, retaining all thirty answers. ## What this means for you

**Reasoned.** Choose the construction role before following a name. A residential builder coordinating jobs, a general contractor managing several parties and an owner overseeing capital projects need different evaluations. Start with the question closest to your work; a larger total can accumulate names from decisions that do not resemble yours.

Write down one operation a candidate must handle and identify the evidence you need to assess it. For estimating or scheduling, that might be a document or demonstration of the specific workflow, with its product edition and conditions recorded. Those are checks for your evaluation. The naming table did not execute the operation or compare delivery outcomes.

Keep the saved wording when tracing an answer's sources. A product can be named without the adjacent capability being supported by the cited passage. Conversely, a publisher may be cited without its own software being offered as an option. This engine table measures names, and it supplies no Perplexity-specific citation rate to substitute for them.

If you publish construction-software information, focus a correction on a demonstrable fact. Retain the answer, the primary source and the disputed statement before revising a page. Verify the served wording after the change and date any later AI observation separately. A zero on the small-builder question is not itself proof of an inaccurate description.
